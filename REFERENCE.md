# JTBD cheat sheet

Every format from Ulwick's [*Jobs to be Done: Theory to Practice*](https://jobs-to-be-done-book.com/), on one page.

## Job statement

```
verb + object of the verb + contextual clarifier
```

*listen to music while on the go* · *cut a piece of wood in a straight line* · *monitor a patient's vital signs*

Rules: solution-free, functional only (no adjectives, no emotions), the customer's perspective, a job, not a situation. Not too narrow (the job is *prepare a hot beverage*, not *boil water*), not too broad (could one team own it end to end?).

## Desired outcome statement

```
direction of improvement + performance metric + object of control + contextual clarifier
```

*Minimize the likelihood that the music sounds distorted when played at high volume*
*Minimize the time it takes to set the angle of the blade, e.g., make a bevel adjustment*

Metrics are usually **time** or **likelihood**. Real markets carry 50 to 150 per job.

## The Universal Job Map: 8 steps

| 1. Define | 2. Locate | 3. Prepare | 4. Confirm |
|---|---|---|---|
| plan, select | gather, access | set up, organize | validate, decide |

| 5. Execute | 6. Monitor | 7. Modify | 8. Conclude |
|---|---|---|---|
| perform | verify, track | adjust, update | finish, close out |

Maps what the customer is *trying to get done*, not what they do with today's product, and not the buying journey (that's consumption-chain territory).

## The six need types

1. Core functional job (the anchor; define it first)
2. Desired outcomes on the core job (50 to 150)
3. Related jobs (5 to 20)
4. Emotional and social jobs (5 to 25)
5. Consumption chain jobs (purchase, install, learn, maintain, dispose)
6. Buyer's financial desired outcomes (40 to 80)

## Opportunity Algorithm

```
opportunity = importance + max(importance - satisfaction, 0)
```

Importance and satisfaction = % of surveyed customers rating 4 or 5 on a 5-point scale, expressed 0 to 10.

| Score | Reading |
|---|---|
| 10 or higher | Underserved. The opportunity. |
| High imp + high sat | Table stakes. Match them; they won't differentiate. |
| Sat > imp | Overserved. Cut cost, or a disruptor's foothold. |

## Growth Strategy Matrix: 5 strategies

| Strategy | Job done... | Wins with |
|---|---|---|
| Differentiated | better, costs more | underserved customers |
| Dominant | better, costs less | everyone |
| Disruptive | worse, costs less | overserved + nonconsumers |
| Discrete | worse, costs more | restricted customers |
| Sustaining | <5% better/cheaper | existing customers |

Customers switch at about **20% better**. Differentiated fails in overserved segments; disruptive fails in underserved ones. Segment first.
